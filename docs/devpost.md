# Devpost draft: Lost in the Line

Paste each section into the matching Devpost field. Every number comes from `results/` and `app/data/app_data.json`.

**Tagline:** A multilingual 311 request can sound understood while losing the one fact staff need. We measured it on
real speech in 14 languages.

## Inspiration
Mississauga's 311 line answers about 1,006 calls a business day, and 24,305 residents cannot hold a conversation in
English or French. Voice AI chains (speech to text, then machine translation) are arriving in city services, and
their quality is usually judged by whether the English "reads well". A request can read well and still lose the
address, the date, the number or the word "not".

## What it does
- **Play the Line:** read a real benchmark sentence in your language; the trace shows the expected vs detected
  language, what the ear heard, the English staff receive beside the human reference, and each critical fact as
  survived, changed or missing. A Benchmark receipt shows the precomputed evidence for that language. Works live,
  or from a verified replay offline.
- **False-Comfort Matrix:** meaning check (AI judge plus embeddings) against critical facts, for every clip.
- **Power-Up Decision Lab:** the same audio with the language supplied up front, plus "would confirmation have
  fired?".
- **Mississauga screening:** benchmark-weighted language exposure by neighbourhood (screening, not a forecast).

## How we built it
- Answer key: FLEURS, the same 40 sentences read by real speakers in 14 languages (560 clips), with the English row
  of the same sentence as the human reference.
- Ear: ElevenLabs Scribe v2, language auto-detected; CER, language detection and script checked separately.
- Translator: Gemini (`gemini-3.8-flash`, temperature 0), twice per clip: from the clean transcript (T1) and from
  what Scribe heard (T2).
- Critical facts by fixed rules, no AI: numbers, dates and times, negation, names and places. We compare each fact
  across the two translations and label it survived, ASR-associated break, translation-baseline break, or
  compensation. For meaning scores we report the incremental degradation after substituting the ASR transcript, not
  a causal share.
- Paired cluster bootstrap by sentence for every interval. Every API response is cached, so re-runs cost nothing.
- Prototype: one offline HTML page reading a JSON file generated by the pipeline; a small Python server adds live
  recording and keeps the API keys off the browser.

## What we found
- **20 of 322 fact-bearing recordings (6.2%, 95% CI 2.8% to 9.6%) looked fine on meaning but lost a critical
  fact.** In 17 of the 20 the fact was lost even from the clean transcript (translation baseline); in 3 it was lost
  only from what Scribe heard.
- **The ear mislabels languages silently.** 7 of 40 Urdu recordings were labelled Hindi and written in Devanagari;
  22 of 40 Cantonese recordings were written in Mainland Simplified script instead of Hong Kong Traditional;
  English: 0 of 40. When language and script were right, CER was 0.026; when wrong, 0.543.
- **Supplying the language helps the label, not the facts.** Urdu went from 82.5% to 100% right language and
  script (CER 0.215 to 0.036); Cantonese only from 40% to 52.5%; critical-fact survival did not measurably change.
- **Confirmation would catch most wrong facts.** For 24 of the 28 recordings with a wrong fact, a readback would have
  shown the caller a fact of the type that went wrong (potential silent failures surfaced for confirmation).
- **Where:** the highest benchmark-weighted exposure is in DT Cooksville, Fairview and Churchill Meadows.

**Recommendation:** check facts, not just fluency; supply the caller's language from the phone menu; add a
confirm-back step for numbers, dates, names and negation before closing a request.

## How we evaluated the evaluator
1. Baselines: English speakers for the ear (0 wrong labels, CER 0.008); T1 for the translator.
2. Three signals: AI judge vs embeddings (Spearman 0.45, n = 1,080) and vs the rule-based fact check (0.10).
   Embedding similarity is compressed (99.4% of clips at or above 0.90), so the 0.85 threshold never changed a cell;
   we report 0.80 and 0.90 as well.
3. Human spot-check: a team member labelled 20 random pairs. The AI judge agreed on 12 of 20, and every
   disagreement went the same way: the human saw a changed message where the judge said same or minor. One rater
   only, so human vs human agreement is not measured.
4. Honest changes: judge prompt v2 before the full run; one model for every final translation and grade; fact rules
   refined once after inspecting examples (double negatives, prefixed negation, "one of", lakh and crore), each
   unit-tested and each reducing false alarms.

## Challenges
Gemini's free tier allows 20 requests a day, so we batched 200 translations per request. One model was closed to new
accounts mid-event. The answer key itself drifts, which we separate out with the two-translation design.

## Limits
Read speech is a best case. Few speakers per language, and several languages are single-sex. Dialect mismatches
(Brazilian Portuguese, Latin American Spanish, Egyptian Arabic). Census counts may not be unique people and are
rounded to 5. Bengali is unmeasured. The screening index is a weighting, not a forecast of failed calls.

## What's next
A pilot on consented City calls with fact checks and a confirm-back step, measured the same way before any vendor
change.
